Security and responsible disclosure

If you think you have found a security problem in Vidyasetu, please tell us privately so that we can fix it before it is made public. This page explains how to report it, what is in scope and which security controls the platform has today.

1. How to report

Security contact
Machine-readable notice
/.well-known/security.txt

In your report, please include:

  • a short description of the problem and what an attacker could do with it;
  • the page, address or feature affected, and the steps to reproduce it;
  • the account type you used (for example a parent account), and whether you needed any special access;
  • your name or handle and a way to contact you. You may report anonymously.

2. What is in scope

  • The web app and the API that serves it, on the address the operator publishes.
  • Sign-in, sessions, password handling and account lockout.
  • Access control: anything that lets one user see another school's, another branch's or another person's data.
  • The public pages and the security headers.

3. What is out of scope

  • Social engineering of staff, parents or students, and any physical attack.
  • Denial-of-service testing, or load tests, against the live service.
  • Automated scanning at volume that slows the service for other users.
  • Problems in third-party services we do not run, which should be reported to their owners.
  • Reports that only show missing best practice with no realistic way to exploit it.

4. Please do this

  • Do not access, change, copy or delete data that is not yours. Use your own test account if you need one.
  • Stop as soon as you have confirmed the problem, and do not look further into other people's data.
  • Do not degrade the service for other users.
  • Keep the details private until we have had a reasonable time to fix the problem.

5. Our commitment

We will acknowledge your report, keep you informed of progress, and credit you if you wish once the problem is fixed. If you act in good faith and follow the rules on this page, we do not intend to take legal action against you for the research. This is a statement of intent, not a legal guarantee, and it does not cover anyone who breaks these rules.

6. Security controls in place today

  • Passwords are hashed with Argon2 before they are stored.
  • Passwords must be at least 10 characters and must not be a commonly used password.
  • Repeated failed sign-ins lock the account for a set time, with limits set by the school.
  • Sign-in and other sensitive requests are rate limited.
  • Accounts created or reset by an administrator must change the password at first sign-in.
  • The access token is kept only in memory. The refresh token is an httpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie and is rotated on use.
  • Sign-in, refresh and sign-out requests must carry a custom request header and an allowed origin, which blocks cross-site form posts.
  • Responses include security headers, including a content security policy and frame blocking.
  • Every request is limited to the signed-in user's school and branch on the server.
  • Error responses are generic and do not reveal whether an account exists.

7. Planned, not yet in place

We have not yet put these in place. We list them so that nobody assumes they exist:

  • an audit log of administrative and sensitive actions (planned);
  • two-factor sign-in for platform and school administrators (planned);
  • database-level row security as a second layer of isolation (planned);
  • encryption of stored data at rest beyond what the hosting provider offers (planned);
  • tested backups and a restore procedure (planned);
  • an independent security review or penetration test (planned, not yet done).
